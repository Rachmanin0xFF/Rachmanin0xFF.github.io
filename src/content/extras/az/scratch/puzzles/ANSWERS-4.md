# Answers, Round Four

1. **Wheelbarrows.**

2. **Burn your own patch of ground.** Light the grass just to the east of
   where you stand. The wind carries that fire away from you and it clears a
   strip down to bare earth. Step onto the burnt ground and let the main fire
   arrive and find nothing to feed on.

3. **"Three."** The password is the number of letters in the word the guard
   says. Twelve has six letters, six has three, ten has three. The spy was
   halving.

4. **Into the air.** Deliberately miss. If you kill the perfect shot, the
   two thirds shooter kills you two times in three. If you kill the two thirds
   shooter, the perfect shot kills you for certain. Miss on purpose and the
   other two must deal with each other first, because each is the other's
   biggest threat. You get the next shot at whoever survives.

5. **Tip a second A out of the bottle**, so you are holding two of each. Cut
   all four pills in half and make two piles, putting one half of every pill
   into each pile. Each pile is now exactly one A and one B. Take one today
   and the other tomorrow.

6. **Move any ten coins into a pile of their own, then flip all ten of them
   over.** If your pile happened to contain some number of heads, the other
   ninety contain the rest. Flipping your ten turns your heads into tails and
   your tails into heads, which leaves exactly as many heads in your pile as
   in the big one. It works no matter which ten you grabbed.

7. **Exactly the same amount of each.** Both glasses end up holding the volume
   they started with. So whatever wine is missing from the wine glass must
   have been replaced by precisely that much water, and that missing wine is
   sitting in the water glass. The stirring, the spoon size, and how well it
   mixed are all irrelevant.

8. **She draws a stone, fumbles it, and drops it on the gravel path**, where
   it is instantly lost among thousands of identical stones. She apologizes,
   and then points out that the colour of the one still in the bag will show
   which one she drew. It is black, so hers was white. The moneylender cannot
   object without admitting what he did.

9. **He was blind.** He walked by feel and used the music to know where he was
   on the rope and when he had reached the far platform. When it stopped he
   lost his place.

10. **Two, two, and nine.** Three ages multiplying to 36 give these sums: 38,
    21, 16, 14, 13, 13, 11, 10. The census taker could see the house number
    and still could not decide, so the number must be 13, the only sum that
    comes up twice, from 1, 6, 6 and from 2, 2, 9. "My eldest" means there is
    a single oldest child, which rules out 1, 6, 6.

11. **Take one coin from the first sack, two from the second, three from the
    third, and so on up to ten from the tenth.** That is 55 coins, which would
    weigh 550 grams if all were genuine. Weigh them. However many grams short
    you are is the number of the counterfeit sack.

12. **She lends them her own camel.** That makes eighteen. The eldest takes
    half, which is nine. The middle takes a third, which is six. The youngest
    takes a ninth, which is two. Nine plus six plus two is seventeen, so her
    camel is left over and she rides away on it. The will's fractions never
    added up to a whole in the first place, which is what caused the problem.
